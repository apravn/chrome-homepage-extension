What Nook is

Nook replaces your new tab page with a clock, web search, quick links, an animated or photo background, and visual boards for saving and organizing pages or text you select while browsing. This policy explains exactly what it stores, why, and where it goes — which, for almost everything, is nowhere but your own device.

sync is synced across your signed‑in Chrome browsers by Google's own Chrome Sync, under your Google account. local stays on this device only. Nook has no server in either path. Boards and cards stay in local storage rather than sync — a board full of saved pages can easily outgrow Chrome sync's small per‑item size limit, so they intentionally don't sync across your devices yet.

How this data reaches Nook

Only through something you did on purpose:

  • Clicking Save current page or Save selected text in the popup
  • Using the right‑click menu items "Save page to Nook" or "Save selection to Nook"
  • Pressing the Quick Save keyboard shortcut
  • Choosing bookmarks to import from your existing Chrome bookmarks
  • Creating, renaming, or deleting a board, adding a quick link, changing a setting, or uploading a background photo

Nook does not watch your browsing in the background, log the pages you visit, track clicks or scrolling, or record keystrokes. It only reads the active tab's title, URL, favicon, or current text selection at the exact moment you trigger one of the actions above.

What Nook never does

  • Never sells or transfers your data to third parties
  • Never uses your data for anything outside Nook's own features
  • Never uses your data to assess creditworthiness or for lending
  • Never collects your name, email, health, financial, authentication, or location data
  • Never reads personal communications (email, chat, messages)

The two outbound requests Nook makes

Favicon icons — to show the little site icon next to a quick link or Inbox item, your browser requests it directly from Google's public favicon service (google.com/s2/favicons), the same way any website's favicon loads. Nook doesn't see or log this request.

Your search — typing in Nook's search bar sends your query straight to whichever engine you picked (Google, Bing, or DuckDuckGo), exactly as if you'd typed it into that site directly. Nook itself never receives or stores your search terms.

Your controls

Every card has a remove button, and can be moved between boards or dragged to reorder. Boards can be created, renamed, or deleted at any time (deleting a board moves its cards to your default board first — nothing is silently discarded). Quick links and the custom background photo can be deleted from Settings at any time. Uninstalling Nook removes all of its locally stored data immediately; synced data clears from your Google account's extension storage as Chrome would for any removed extension.

Children's privacy

Nook is not directed at children and does not knowingly collect data from anyone under 13.

Permissions Nook requests

storage
Read and write the data described above via chrome.storage.
unlimitedStorage
Lets an uploaded background photo exceed Chrome's default 10MB local storage cap.
contextMenus
Adds the "Save page / selection to Nook" right‑click menu items.
activeTab
Reads the active tab's title, URL, and favicon only when you click Save.
scripting
Reads your current text selection only when you click "Save selected text" in the popup.
bookmarks
Read‑only — lets the Import panel show your existing Chrome bookmarks so you can copy some onto a board. Nook never creates, edits, or removes anything in your actual bookmarks.

The Quick Save keyboard shortcut is registered via Chrome's commands API, which doesn't require a separate permission — you can view or change its key combination anytime at chrome://extensions/shortcuts.
